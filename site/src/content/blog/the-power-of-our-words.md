If the three great virtues produced in a believer’s life are faith,  hope, and love, our speech and words may be the most powerful tool for exercising those virtues. Proverbs has a lot to say about the  power of our words.  Consider this small sampling.

## The Power of Our Words to Heal

> Gracious words are a honeycomb, sweet to the soul and healing to the  bones (Proverbs 16:24).

## The Power of Our Words to Stir Up Trouble

> A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger (Proverbs 15:1).

What you don’t say is as important as what you do say:

> Even fools are thought wise if they keep silent, and discerning if they hold their tongues (Proverbs 17:28).

## The Power of Our Words to Bring Life and Death

> The tongue has the power of life and death, and those who love it will eat its fruit (Proverbs 18:21).

As you prepare for Sunday, reflect on the following proverbs; allow them to constructively critique your words and reveal what they are producing.

## 1. Wounding or Healing?

> The words of the reckless pierce like swords, but the tongue of the wise brings healing (Proverbs 12:18).

Reflective Focus: Think about specific interactions with family, coworkers, or online. Reflect on Proverbs 12:18 and ask yourself whether your tone and timing brought life and encouragement, or if reckless comments left subtle wounds.

## 2. The Discipline of Silence

> Sin is not ended by multiplying words, but the prudent hold their tongues  (Proverbs 10:19).

Reflective Focus: Using Proverbs 10:19 and 17:28, consider whether you tend to over-explain, gossip, or rush to defend yourself when misunderstood. Identify one area where practicing intentional silence or waiting to reply could protect you from regret.

## 3. De-escalation vs. Fueling the Fire

> A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.  (Proverbs 15:1).

Reflective Focus: Think about how you respond to criticism or a harsh tone from others. As you refelect on Proverbs 15:1, evaluate whether your default reaction stirs up anger or actively diffuses tension.  Are you a thermometer that simply reflects the heat of a tense conversation, or are you a thermostat that helps cool it down?
